# Medical Center Modernization Service  
**Execution Plan**

---

# Phase 1 (Weeks 1–2): Problem Proof, Not Product

### Goal
Confirm **who hurts**, **why**, and **how badly** — in their words.

---


### 1. Identify Who to Talk To
Create a list of **30 people**, aim to speak to **10**.

**Target roles**
- Clinic owner
- Medical director
- Operations / practice manager
- Nursing home administrator

**Target clinic types**
- Small private clinics (5–30 staff)
- Nursing homes
- Rural or understaffed clinics

---

### 2. Prepare a Pain-Focused Interview Script
Do **not** pitch yet.

Ask:
- What breaks most often during a normal week?
- Where do staff lose the most time?
- What causes overtime, sick leave, or complaints?
- Who manages IT, scheduling, and tools today?
- What happens when someone is sick unexpectedly?
- What does this chaos cost you monthly (roughly)?

---

### 3. Inventory Existing Tools (Without Judgement)
Document what they already use:
- Email / WhatsApp / paper
- Scheduling software
- EHR (even if terrible)
- Hardware situation

This informs automation later.

---

## Output

Write **one single sentence ICP**:

> “We help **[type of clinic]** with **[X–Y staff]** reduce operational chaos and burnout without hiring IT or ops staff.”

If you can’t write this clearly → you’re not ready to proceed.

---

## Validation Signal

You hear phrases like:
- “This is a constant problem”
- “We’ve tried software but…”
- “It always falls on the same people”
- “We don’t have time to manage this”

If you hear polite interest but no pain → wrong ICP.

---

# Phase 2 (Weeks 2–4): Sell Before You Build

### 🎯 Goal
Prove someone will **pay**, not just agree.

---

## What to Do

### 1. Create a 1-Page Pitch (No Website)
This is a PDF or doc, not marketing fluff.

**Structure**
- The chaos you see in clinics
- Why software alone fails
- Your promise: operational stability
- One simple offer
- One simple price anchor

---

### 2. Pitch 10 Decision-Makers
Now you pitch.

Say:
> “We take clinics with no IT or ops capacity and give them a fully functioning operational system in 30 days.”

Then stop talking.

---

## What You Are Testing

- Does the problem feel urgent?
- Does the price feel reasonable?
- Do they ask “how soon can this start?”

---

## Validation Signal

Someone says:
> “If you can actually do this, we’d pay.”

No contracts yet. Just **verbal willingness**.

---

# Phase 3 (Weeks 4–6): Define the First Paid Offer

### 🎯 Goal
Create **one productized service**, not a platform.

---

## Your First Offer

**Name**
> **Managed Operations for Clinics with No IT**

---

## Included (Strictly Limited)
- IT support (devices, access, accounts)
- Communication setup (chat, calls, email)
- Scheduling & shift coverage logic
- Basic operational reporting

Nothing else.

---

## Pricing (Anchor)
- **€40–60 per staff / month**
- Minimum monthly contract (e.g. €1,000)

> If this price makes *you* uncomfortable, it’s probably correct.

---

## Validation Signal

At least **1 clinic agrees to pilot**:
- Paid
- Small
- Real

No free pilots.

---

# Phase 4 — 30-Day Fully Automated Modernization  
**NO MANUAL OPERATIONS**

### 🎯 Goal
Deliver a **repeatable, productized transformation** — not consulting.

---

## Design Rule (Non-Negotiable)

- No custom setups
- No hero employees
- No ongoing manual work

Everything must be:
- Template-driven
- Rule-based
- Automated
- Repeatable

Humans only:
- Monitor
- Escalate
- Improve templates

---

# Phase 5 — Scale & Defensibility (After 3–5 Clients)

### Goal
Become infrastructure, not software.

---

## Actions

- Compliance profiles

---

### Build Proprietary Assets

- Staffing intelligence
- Burnout prediction models

---

### Lock-In
- Hardware lifecycle
- Operational data
- Deep workflow dependency

---

## Long-Term Positioning

> “We are the operating system for understaffed medical centers.”
